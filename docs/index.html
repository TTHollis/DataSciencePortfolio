About

I spent fifteen years at Citibank, in roles that included supervising a client escalations team and teaching fraud mitigation as an AVP trainer. Today I work in a bank contact center while finishing a master's degree in data science.

The fraud work on this site started with a job search, not a classroom. When that job ended during COVID, I spent months reading postings, and too many of them were too good to be true. I wanted to know whether the warning signs I was catching by eye were consistent enough for a model to learn. Three projects later, the answer is a free tool anyone can use.

I am drawn to problems where a data decision lands on a real person: a job seeker deciding whether to trust a posting, a family working out where they can afford to live, a homeowner whose appraisal comes in low.

I write up what did not work as plainly as what did. The Billboard project is on this page because auditing my own result turned an impressive number into a correct one. Every write-up is meant to be followed by someone without a data background and still hold up for someone with one.

One problem, three passes: fraudulent job postings

Each project exists because of something the one before it could not do. All three now run together in one live tool.

  1. Job Posting Fraud Classifier

    Can a model tell a scam posting from a real one?

    Yes. The chosen model catches 156 of 173 scams in the test set. It was picked over a model with better scores on paper, because that one missed 61 scams.

  2. Verify This Job

    Can it explain why a posting looks wrong?

    A fine-tuned language model gives a verdict and names the red flags. A separate set of rules runs beside it, so one bad model call cannot wave a scam through.

  3. Scam Signatures

    What does fraud look like when nothing is labeled?

    Grouping postings by language alone found one operator running 99 postings off a single reused paragraph. A classifier that had never seen that campaign caught only 14.1 percent of it.

Try Verify This Job

One goal, three passes: finding a place to call home

Each project zooms in from the one before it, from counties to neighborhoods to what a home there is worth. The third was built to plug back into the second.

  1. City Livability Data Pipeline

    Where can a family afford to live and still get around?

    Rent, walkability, and Census data never line up geographically. Reconciling them produced a livability score for 2,309 counties, but a county is too big to tell you much about a street.

  2. QRoots

    What about the neighborhood, not just the county?

    Scores 85,396 census tracts on six dimensions, including LGBTQ+ inclusion, and lets you reweight them to match what matters to you. Every score explains what drives it.

  3. Priced Out of Equity

    Is a home there valued fairly?

    Homes in majority-Black neighborhoods are appraised about 21 percent below comparable homes in the same county, and the legacy of 1930s redlining still shows in today's values. Its neighborhood table was built for QRoots and contains no racial data.
